DISCOVERY Q&A
=============

Q1: Were the demos for the 30 SEs custom-built or general product walkthroughs?
A: The 30 SE interactions are primarily customer environment/use case discovery, general product capability walkthroughs, and demos of the Python SDK and dashboards. The 6-8 high-touch accounts are where custom-built automation scripts, dashboards, and solutions are created for specific customers.

Q2: Did any of the high-touch custom engagements directly unblock or close a deal?
A: Deal signature details were obfuscated by the SEs. However, Lucas was brought in on several customer interactions to dynamically demo specific use cases tailored to a customer's networking concerns. Impact is positive but not directly quantifiable to a deal close.

Q3: Did you translate customer requirements into specs or roadmap inputs for engineering?
A: Yes. Customer API feature requests from calls are logged and communicated directly with API Product Lifecycle Management and Engineering as requirements for future product enhancements.

Q4: Did you ever push back on what was feasible and scope a realistic alternative?
A: Yes. A customer on a legacy product wanted a configuration use case that wasn't possible on that version. After significant back-and-forth to reach mutual understanding of requirements, the team was honest that the legacy product couldn't support it but the newer version could. This conversation resulted in a reframing of what the customer purchased from HPE.

Q5: What documentation platforms has HPE used, and what friction have you experienced?
A: HPE has used Swagger (open source) and currently uses Readme.io. Key friction points with Readme:
  - Scale issues: one networking product had so many APIs it had to be split into two separate Readme projects (monitoring/reporting/troubleshooting vs. configuration)
  - OAS file size: configuration API OAS files were so large that Readme's UI couldn't handle rendering them, causing platform-level issues

Q6: Were the Postman collections intentionally designed to be scalable, or did they grow organically?
A: Originally built for a single non-technical customer who had no idea where to start with APIs. The collection and accompanying guides walked a zero-experience user through discovering and testing APIs for their automation use cases. Recognized that many customers share this problem, so the single-customer solution was scaled out to benefit many others across a wide technical spectrum (non-technical users through highly technical IT personnel).

Q7: Did you write technical specs or project plans that engineering built against? Did you ever push back on timelines?
A: Yes. Scoped SDK modules and managed development timelines for an automation team of 5. Pushed back on timelines when APIs in beta phase had errors that needed to be fixed before workflows could be published. Filed bugs directly with Engineering and coordinated fixes before releasing automation to customers.

Q8: How fast can you build a functional prototype? What's the most you've pulled off under time pressure?
A: Can go from zero to working demo within 48 hours. Example: a customer wanted to see a floor-plan AP visualization. Lucas had never worked with those API endpoints before but added the visualization to an existing dashboard demo. The customer was genuinely shocked by the quality and speed of delivery - they were not expecting a demo at all for that call.

Q9: Can you give an example of adjusting your communication for different audiences mid-engagement?
A: Yes. Common scenario: C-suite executives on a call who eventually drop off and hand over to their IT department. This requires leading with high-level executive-facing value while maintaining enough technical depth to shift gears immediately when the IT team takes over. Discovery calls involve a lot of "vibe sensing" to understand use cases, whether a customer is single or multi-vendor, their level of interest in specific product areas, and where to expand their footprint.

Q10: Did you build something that scaled your impact beyond your own direct involvement?
A: Yes. Built a full-stack network monitoring demo environment: Next.js custom dashboard UI + Grafana + InfluxDB (time series DB) + Docker. Originally built for a single customer, then scoped out to fit larger campus and branch customers, then open-sourced on GitHub. Created a YouTube demo walkthrough. Multiple sales engineers independently reached out to get the demo running on their own machines for customer demos. Customers saw the YouTube video and asked how to get it. Turned a single customer solution into a repeatable self-service SE sales tool.
